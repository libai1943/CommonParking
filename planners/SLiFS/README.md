# Successive linearization in feasible sets (SLiFS)

Chaoyi Sun, Qing Li, Bai Li and Li Li, *A Successive Linearization in Feasible Set Algorithm for Vehicle Motion Planning in Unstructured and Low-Speed Scenarios*, IEEE Transactions on Intelligent Transportation Systems 23(4), 3724–3736, 2022 (online 2021), DOI [10.1109/TITS.2020.3041075](https://doi.org/10.1109/TITS.2020.3041075).

This independent MATLAB implementation follows the paper's two-loop Algorithm 1, L1 penalty QP, circle-centre feasible sets and first-step perturbation. It is not an NLP with the same name. The experiments in the article used CPLEX for the quadratic subproblems; this implementation uses MATLAB `quadprog` (`interior-point-convex`). It therefore requires Optimization Toolbox and does not claim to reproduce the authors' solver timings.

## Formulation

The variables are rear-axle XY, heading, signed speed, front-wheel steering, the XY centre of the foremost covering circle, and the free final time. There are 200 uniformly timed states (199 intervals), an explicit implementation mesh choice. The common bicycle model is discretized by forward Euler, as in Eqs. (6)–(8). The nonlinear vehicle-shape equation relates the foremost centre to rear-axle XY and heading; all other circle centres are affine combinations of these positions, exactly as in Eqs. (10)–(11).

The original objective, Eq. (1), is `sum(diff(v).^2) + sum(diff(phi).^2) + tf`. These are squared differences, without a time-step multiplier or division. The three weights are one. The fixed L1 penalty is `lambda=1e5`. Every QP introduces nonnegative epigraph variables for the absolute values of the linearized kinematic/shape equalities. The complete QP objective is divided by lambda for numerical conditioning, preserving its minimizers and relative weights.

Speed and steering bounds are hard constraints. The two difference-rate inequalities, `abs(diff(v)) <= amax*tf/199` and `abs(diff(phi)) <= omegaMax*tf/199`, are also hard and linear. Start and goal XY/heading are fixed; speed and steering are zero at both ends. The selected unwrapped terminal heading represents the required orientation modulo 2*pi. Returned accelerations and steering rates are the interval differences divided by the actual time step, with zero at the last sample.

## Collision geometry and iteration

Table I uses five equally spaced circles along the longitudinal centreline, each circumscribing its corresponding full-width rectangular cell. The radius is `sqrt((length/10)^2+(width/2)^2)`. The paper's 0.1 m safety margin is retained. A required endpoint blocked by that conservative cover is an explicit method failure, even if the original rectangle fits the scene. The requested two-dimensional multi-disc extension for STC/LIOM is not applied to this method.

For each current circle centre and each convex obstacle, the closest boundary point supplies a supporting halfplane. It is offset by the circle radius plus the safety margin. All circle/obstacle constraints are retained. The paper's optional elimination of irrelevant obstacles is not required here. The halfplanes are fixed throughout an inner loop, then rebuilt in the outer loop. The NLP equalities are relinearized at every inner iteration. An infinity-norm trust region bounds changes in every variable, including final time.

The first QP trial in every inner loop is accepted even if the nonlinear penalized merit increases: this is the perturbation described in Section III-D. Subsequent trials follow the paper's actual-merit test, backtracking when needed, and its ratio of linearized to nonlinear equality penalties. The trust region shrinks or grows using the Table I values. At the end of inner loops, feasible candidates are compared by the original objective, and the best is retained. A failure in the native QP, lack of convergence within the resource limit, or a final infeasible candidate remains failure.

After optimization, the between-waypoint check in Appendix B is implemented using polygon intersection: the convex hull of adjacent footprints conservatively contains the linear corner sweeps. Additional interpolated footprints are checked at a translation-plus-rotation step no larger than 0.01 m. This extra sampling handles changing orientation and is a disclosed numerical check, not a claim of certified continuous collision avoidance. The common evaluator's execution replay remains a separate measurement.

## Initialization and disclosed numerical choices

Remark 3 explicitly permits replacing the coarse eight-neighbor A* initializer with Hybrid A*. This version uses that permitted alternative, with the common HA search settings and without the CG smoother. The result receives the common rest-to-rest longitudinal timing and is resampled onto the SLiFS mesh. Initial duration is at least the paper's 20 s; a longer rest-to-rest duration is retained. Speed and steering are clipped and swept forward/backward to meet difference-rate limits and zero endpoints. The article's arcsine-based Eq. (19) is not used because the HA initializer already supplies headings and gear directions.

Retained Table I parameters are `lambda=1e5`, `epsilon1=1e-3`, `epsilon2=1e-2`, `epsilon3=1`, `r0=4`, `rLower=1e-3`, `rho1=0.2`, `rho2=0.9`, `alpha=beta=2.5`, and the five circles with 0.1 m margin. Common vehicle dimensions and motion limits replace the article's example vehicle. Resource limits are 1,500 QPs, 200 wall seconds including initialization, and at most 100 iterations per inner loop. Final time is restricted to `[0.1,100]` s. Each QP allows 300 iterations, primal/optimality tolerance 1e-9 and step tolerance 1e-12. MATLAB numerical threads are temporarily set to one and restored on return.

Several pseudocode details need an executable interpretation. The printed outer-loop inequality would loop on convergence; this implementation stops when the change is below `N*epsilon1`, consistent with the accompanying convergence description. The first-step `continue` advances the inner counter, avoiding an endless sequence of first steps. The unspecified line search uses up to twenty halvings. The unspecified small trust radius in line 25 is 0.1. The epsilon2 update is retained, with a positive floating-point floor. Equalities are accepted when their maximum absolute discrete residual is below 1e-3, the feasibility threshold stated in the text. These details are exposed rather than attributed to values absent from the paper.

`RunPlanner('SLiFS',caseId)` returns a trajectory and the native QP flags, outer/inner history, nonlinear residuals and solver convergence flag. Failed candidates may still be returned for diagnosis. `TestSLiFS` verifies analytic Jacobians against independent directional differences, exact rectangle signed distances, support halfplanes, the quadratic objective, and a swept collision missed by checking only endpoints.
